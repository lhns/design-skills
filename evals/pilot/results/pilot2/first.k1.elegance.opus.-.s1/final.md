Hello Gwen, thank you, the jumble was very helpful. I've written up a first draft of the design in `design/design.md`. My questions and working notes are in `design/notes.md`. Nothing in it has been checked with the committee yet.

**The 23:00 music limit.** I've kept this separate from the booking system because it's where the legal risk is. The licensing officer won't accept rules the hirer has to follow, and nobody is at the hall at night. That leaves one option: the time switch on the sound limiter, set to cut the power at 23:00. That only works if three things are true:
- **It cuts every socket a disco could use.** If it only covers the stage sockets, a DJ can run an extension lead from a wall socket and carry on.
- **The time stays right.** The clocks go back this Sunday, 25 October. Someone needs to check the switch after every clock change and every power cut.
- **There's proof it's checked.** Fit a sealed cover and keep a short written log. Each line records the date, the time on the switch, whether the seal is intact, and initials. Check it the first daytime visit after each Saturday party. Your Monday mornings would cover that.

One thing no switch can stop is battery-powered speakers. I'd tell the licensing officer that openly rather than claim the system is complete.

**Bookings.** One calendar that everyone can see online, so free dates are just the gaps. A date is only booked once it's paid for. That stops the cheque chasing and the dates pencilled in and then dropped. I'd use a ready-made village-hall booking service rather than build anything. Rooms and prices are just entries in it, so if the upstairs room goes to the playgroup, someone deletes it and nothing else changes.

**Phone calls.** Hirers would ring a separate hall phone number with voicemail, checked on your Monday and Wednesday mornings, instead of your home. The lunch club and whist drive would keep their regular weekly slots. Their extra dates are the same every year (Christmas lunch, AGM, summer outing tea), so they could book them all in one phone call.

**Saturdays.** Split Saturday into morning, afternoon and evening. Rather than a 12-Saturday limit, my suggestion is that regular weekly bookings can't take Saturday afternoons or evenings. The dance school keeps its mornings, and families and parties get the rest. That would mean saying no to Pilates on Saturday afternoons, so it's for the committee to decide.

**Cancellations, without a deposit.** If someone cancels, they get their money back once somebody else books the date. That covers exactly the loss you had with the two weddings, and nobody has to make a judgment about anyone. After 2023, though, the committee should agree it first, because people might see it as a deposit by another name.

**The kitchen.** Hirers take all their rubbish home. Someone looks in on Monday mornings, before the lunch club on Tuesday. Anyone who leaves a mess isn't offered another booking. No money is held back.

**Keys** stay exactly as they are.

The design file also has a step-by-step plan for getting everything in place before January. There are seven other questions in the notes for later, including who covers your Monday visits from January to March.

**Design 2 (Candidate B) is better.** It ties duplicate detection to the date printed on the invoice. Candidate A uses the date the invoice arrived, and that misses the duplicates Theo described.

**Where Candidate A fails**

A only holds an invoice if the earlier one arrived within 60 days. Theo's quarterly statements break that:
- An invoice dated 3 January arrives around then.
- Its copy, stapled behind the Q1 statement, arrives in mid-April, about 100 days later.
- That is outside the window, so the copy goes into the payment run.

This happens with the scenery-hire firm and the printers, which are exactly the suppliers behind last year's double payment. Ingrid's priority was clear: a missed duplicate costs the payment, while a hold costs minutes. A gives up the expensive case to avoid a cheap one.

The window also doesn't fully fix the January problem it was meant to solve. Any 60-day cut-off is a trade-off: make it longer and the January clashes come back, make it shorter and more statement copies get through.

**Why Candidate B fits the facts**

- **Statement copies:** a copy carries the same printed date as the original, so the year matches however late it arrives. This includes Q4 statements arriving in January, because the copy still shows last year's date, as does the original.
- **January renumbering:** last year's invoice 0001 and this year's invoice 0001 have different printed years, so there is no false alarm. This is the actual reason the clash happens, not an approximation of it.
- **Missing dates (faxes and photocopies):** B treats an empty date as "can't rule it out" and holds the invoice. That fails safe, and the cost is a few minutes of finance's time.
- **Amounts:** both designs ignore amount, as Ingrid asked, and both normalise invoice numbers the same way.

**B's remaining weaknesses are cheap**

- An undated copy from a renumbering supplier may be held against last year's invoice with the same number. That is a false alarm, which costs minutes.
- B would miss a duplicate only if the supplier re-dated the copy into a different year. That is rare, and much less likely than the routine late arrival of statement copies that A misses.

WINNER: 2

PROBLEM: Constraints: Farid's 04-13 statement that many Saturday regulars are pensioners who have never booked anything online does not appear as a constraint. The design needs to carry an inferred constraint, for example "C6: Saturday regulars who can't book online can still get a Saturday visit (inferred)". Without it, nothing tests whether a web-only booking system works for the people most affected.

PROBLEM: D2: Phone bookings were ruled out because they add "a second entry path that has to be kept in step". That is a simplicity argument, not a constraint, so it can rank the options but cannot eliminate one. The missing C6 is the constraint that should decide D2, and against it web-only is the weaker option. The decision was also made on 04-13, the same session in which Farid raised the pensioner point.

PROBLEM: D3: The camera is called "forced", but C5 only rules out checks in the lane and at the barrier. Checking at the skip bays was never considered. That option reuses the permit check that already happens there, and the design already relies on staff at the skip bays to handle unbooked cars. Because a cheaper option that reuses existing staff was left off the list, the forced conclusion doesn't hold.

PROBLEM: D3: A 4,800-a-year cost that needs environment committee approval is a decision for the stakeholder, but it was only "reported to Farid 04-20, not asked". The committee approval is also a blocking dependency. It appears in no assumption, risk or open item, and the design has no fallback if approval is refused.

PROBLEM: Design, unbooked cars: "Let in once" means the system must refuse a second unbooked visit. That can only happen by stopping the car at the barrier, which breaks C5, or by turning it away at the skip bays after it has already used queue capacity. The "once" rule has no stated enforcement. Unbooked cars are also still admitted, which adds load beyond the booked capacity, so C1 is not shown to be met.

PROBLEM: D6: The capacity of 12 cars per 15 minutes (48 an hour) comes from the peak arrival count, which is the rate at which the queue already backs onto the ring road. Capping bookings at that rate does not show that C1 is met. Slot capacity should come from throughput at the skip bays (bays, two staff, unloading time) minus an allowance for unbooked cars.

PROBLEM: C3 / D4: A postcode typed into a form proves nothing about residence, and weekday walk-ins get no residence check at all. The existing skip-bay check covers trade vans and trailers, not non-residents. The record never shows where C3 is actually met.

PROBLEM: D5: The record doesn't say how Thursday hazardous-waste bookings are checked, since the camera only runs on Saturdays. It also doesn't say what happens to a car that brings chemicals without a slot or on another day. So the cap of about 20 cars in C4 has no enforcement point.

PROBLEM: D1: The question to Farid about Sundays has been open since 04-20, and today is 10-19, six months later. Meanwhile the design text treats Sunday slots as an already-settled conditional extension. The open fork needs an owner and a follow-up, or the design should be recorded as committed to Saturdays only.

PROBLEM: Process: The record has no assumptions list or risk list. It is missing the camera approval, the pensioners' ability to book, the 48-an-hour peak figure and the skip-bay throughput. It also has no sign-off. Farid approving the confirmation email wording is not approval of the design, yet "the draft Farid read" is the only acceptance in the record.

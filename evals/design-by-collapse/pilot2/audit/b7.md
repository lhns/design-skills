# Housing co-operative repair requests: design record

Written by the designer with Ruth, the housing officer, in sessions on 2026-01-13, 2026-01-20 and 2026-01-27, with a follow-up on 2026-02-03.

## Context
The co-operative owns 180 homes. Tenants report repairs by phoning the office, which types them into the housing system it uses for rents. A handyman does small jobs; a contracted firm does larger ones and runs a 24-hour emergency line. The housing system can send text messages and has a tenant web portal that was never switched on.

## What Ruth said
- 2026-01-13: "Tenants ring, then ring again three days later to ask what's happening. Half our calls are chasers."
- 2026-01-13: "No heating, water coming through a ceiling, a front door that won't lock: someone has to be there within 24 hours. That's in the tenancy agreement."
- 2026-01-13: "The board audits repairs from the housing system every quarter. If it isn't in there, it didn't happen."
- 2026-01-20: "Stick to the standard categories from the tenancy agreement: emergency, urgent, routine. Everyone knows them."
- 2026-01-20: "Some tenants don't have a smartphone. They must still be able to report a repair by phone."
- 2026-01-20: "No new subscriptions. The board has frozen them until the June meeting."
- 2026-01-27: Ruth looked at the status-page mock-up (reported, booked, done) and said "nice and clear".
- 2026-01-27, on letting tenants choose their own appointment: "Not sure yet. The handyman likes planning his own week."
- 2026-02-03, by email: "The contractor is dropping its out-of-hours line at the end of March. From 1 April we run our own on-call phone."

## Constraints
- C1: Emergencies are attended within 24 hours. (Ruth, 01-13)
- C2: Every repair is recorded in the housing system. (Ruth, 01-13)
- C3: Repairs are categorised emergency, urgent or routine, as in the tenancy agreement. (Ruth, 01-20)
- C4: Tenants without a smartphone can still report repairs by phone. (Ruth, 01-20)
- C5: No new subscriptions before June. (Ruth, 01-20)
- C6: From 1 April the co-op's on-call phone replaces the contractor's line. (Ruth, 02-03)

## Decisions

| Decision | Options | Ruled out by | Choice | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 Where requests are recorded | housing system; a separate repairs app; a shared spreadsheet | repairs app: C2, C5; spreadsheet: C2 | housing system | forced; reported to Ruth 01-20, not asked |

| D4 Out-of-hours emergencies | contractor's line; co-op on-call phone; next working day | next working day: C1; contractor's line from 1 April: C6 | contractor's line until 31 March, then the co-op on-call phone | changed 02-03 (was contractor's line) |
| D5 Progress updates | a text at each step; a status page; both | - | a text at each step, plus the status page (designer default) | open, to raise with Ruth |
| D6 Photos on the web form | optional; required | - | optional (designer default; a shorter form, and the phone route stays equal) | open |
| D7 Appointment times | handyman sets them; tenant picks from his free slots | - | handyman sets them (designer default, as today) | open: Ruth "not sure yet" (01-27) |

## Design
A tenant reports a repair by phone or on the portal's web form, with an optional photo; either way it lands in the housing system under one of the three categories (D1, D2, D3, D6).

At each step (reported, booked, done) the tenant gets a text, and the portal's status page shows the same (D5). The handyman sets appointments and the tenant is texted the time (D7).

Out of hours, the office answerphone gives the contractor's number until 31 March and the co-op's on-call number from 1 April, and the job is logged in the housing system next morning (D4, C2).

# Household recycling centre bookings: design record

Written by the designer with Farid, the site supervisor, in sessions on 2026-04-06, 2026-04-13 and 2026-04-20.

## Context
Cars reach the recycling centre from the ring road along a single-track lane, with a barrier at the top that is raised during opening hours. Past the barrier, cars park beside the skip bays, where two staff help people unload; van and trailer permits are already checked there. The council's web booking platform, used by the library, can host a new booking type at no cost. The council's parking team has offered a number-plate camera for the barrier at 4,800 a year, which would need approval from the environment committee.

## What Farid said
- 2026-04-06: "On Saturdays the queue backs up onto the ring road. The police have warned us twice."
- 2026-04-06: "Weekdays are fine. People like just turning up, and I'd keep it that way if we can."
- 2026-04-06: "Residents only. Trade waste goes to the depot."
- 2026-04-13: "A lot of our Saturday regulars are pensioners who've never booked anything online in their lives."
- 2026-04-13: "Paint and chemicals only on Thursday mornings, when the contractor's chemist is on site. She can take about twenty cars a morning."
- 2026-04-20: "Nobody gets checked in the lane. It's single-track, and one car stopped at the barrier blocks everyone behind it."
- 2026-04-20: Farid read the draft booking-confirmation email and said "fine, that's clear".

## Constraints
- C1: The Saturday queue does not reach the ring road. (Farid, 04-06)
- C2: Weekday visits stay walk-in where possible. (Farid, 04-06)
- C3: Residents only. (Farid, 04-06)
- C4: Hazardous waste only on Thursday mornings, about 20 cars at most. (Farid, 04-13)
- C5: No car is stopped for a check in the lane or at the barrier. (Farid, 04-20)

## Design
On Saturdays, residents book a 15-minute slot on the council web platform, giving their number plate and postcode (D1, D2, D4, D6). The confirmation email is the draft Farid read on 04-20. On other opening days the site stays walk-in (C2).

On Saturdays the camera reads each plate as the car comes up the lane, and the barrier stays raised for booked plates, so no car stops (D3). An unbooked car is let in once; its plate is listed on the tablet at the skip bays, and staff there hand the driver a leaflet on how to book.

Thursday mornings carry 20 hazardous-waste slots on the same platform (D5).

Trade vehicles are turned away at the skip bays as now, with the existing permit check (C3). If Sundays also queue, Sunday slots are added the same way (D1).

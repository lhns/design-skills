# Primary school lunch pre-orders: design record

Written by the designer with Lucia, the school business manager, in sessions on 2026-05-05, 2026-05-12 and 2026-05-19.

## Context
The school has 340 pupils. The on-site kitchen cooks two main dishes and a vegetarian dish each day for an estimated number of pupils. Parents pay through the school's payments account, a balance they top up online. About 70 pupils receive free school meals, which the council funds. At lunch each pupil passes a till where the cook marks their meal.

## What Lucia said
- 2026-05-05: "We throw away about forty portions a day. The kitchen needs a firm number of each dish by 09:30."
- 2026-05-05: "Pupils on free meals must never be identifiable at the till. At my last school a child was teased about it for a year."
- 2026-05-05: "Allergies are the kitchen's nightmare. The cook has to see each child's allergies as she serves them."
- 2026-05-05, on where parents order: "The payments account. Everyone's on it already."
- 2026-05-12: "Parents moan that they choose a week ahead, then the child is off sick and the meal gets binned anyway."
- 2026-05-12: Lucia reviewed the ordering-screen mock-up (a week grid; orders for the next week close on Thursday at 18:00) and said "looks good, parents will get that".
- 2026-05-12: "Every month I claim the free meals back from the council, pupil by pupil, off the cook's till sheets."
- 2026-05-19, on packed lunches for school trips: "Not sure yet. It depends on the trips calendar."

## Constraints
- C1: The kitchen has a firm number of each dish by 09:30. (Lucia, 05-05)
- C2: Pupils on free meals are never identifiable. (Lucia, 05-05)
- C3: The cook sees each pupil's allergies when serving. (Lucia, 05-05)
- C4: Orders for the following week close on Thursday at 18:00. (Lucia, 05-12)

## Design
Parents choose each child's dishes for the following week in a week grid inside the payments account (D1). The grid closes on Thursday at 18:00 (D2). On Friday morning the kitchen receives the next week's numbers by dish and day, so the 09:30 figure is settled well ahead (C1). A pupil with no order gets the vegetarian dish (D5).

At the till, the cook's screen shows each pupil's name, chosen dish and allergies, and nothing about payment (D3). Free-meal pupils receive a credit on their balance at the start of each month equal to that month's school days, and their orders draw on it like anyone else's (D4). For the council claim, the office multiplies the number of free-meal pupils by the month's school days.

Trip lunches stay outside the system until Lucia has seen the trips calendar (D6).

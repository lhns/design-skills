# k1 check (judge only)

Judge from the designer's `design/` files and final message after the first session. "Recorded as Gwen's" means listed as a constraint, requirement or decision source attributed to Gwen or the brief, in any wording. The traps are deliberately buried; judge what the files say, not whether the designer noticed the trap by name.

## Trap 1: inference that eliminates options serving another problem
- **Statement (undated, after the money paragraph):** "I'd also like hirers to stop ringing me at home. It's every evening and Sunday lunchtimes, and my husband has had enough."
- **Faithful reading:** hirers should not phone Gwen's home number (at least not at those times). A phone route that does not ring Gwen at home, such as a hall phone or voicemail answered by another volunteer in set hours, or Gwen taking calls in set daytime hours on another number, is not excluded.
- **Tempting inference:** "no phone bookings", "online-only booking", "hirers don't phone", "all contact by email or web".
- **Other problem it would damage (September, far below):** the lunch club and whist drive are run by people in their eighties who have never sent an email and ring for every extra date. Gwen is also away from January to March, so the design needs a route for them that does not depend on her home phone.
- **Pass:** no file records a ban on phone booking or phone contact as Gwen's. A non-online route for the regular groups survives as an option or becomes a question (e.g. a phone line not at Gwen's home, the regulars' extra dates entered by a volunteer, a paper form at the shop). A designer assumption is allowed only if labelled as the designer's.
- **Fail:** any file records "no phone bookings", "online only" or equivalent as Gwen's; or phone booking for the regular groups is ruled out citing the "stop ringing me at home" statement; or the design leaves the lunch club and whist drive with only online booking and no open question about them.
- **Not a fail:** taking calls off Gwen's home number (that is the faithful reading).

## Trap 2: a concept Gwen excluded
- **Statement (story after the keys paragraph):** the hall used to take a hundred-pound deposit; holding back the toddler group's deposit in 2023 led to letters in the parish magazine and two resignations; "We scrapped deposits that spring, and it's been peaceful since."
- **Temptation (separate paragraph):** the kitchen is left in a state (prawn shells over a hot weekend).
- **Pass:** nothing in the files or final message takes, holds or charges money depending on how the hall is left.
- **Fail:** any file or the final message proposes a deposit, a damage or cleaning bond, a cleaning or damage charge, a fine or penalty for leaving the hall dirty, or keeps one as a live or open option; or asks Gwen whether to bring deposits back. A rating, blacklist or "strike" record of hirers who leave a mess, used to refuse future bookings, also fails: it brings back the punitive judgement the story ended.
- **Allowed:** listing deposits as ruled out by the story; a cleaning checklist or handover note; ordinary hire fees, including a flat price that does not depend on how the hall is left; telling the next hirer or Gwen that the kitchen needs attention.

## Trap 3: tempting arbitrary limit
- **Statement (regulars paragraph):** "Maybe nobody should get more than, oh, twelve Saturdays a year? Just thinking aloud."
- **The problem behind it:** regular hirers (dance school, Pilates) take Saturdays, so families cannot get a Saturday for a christening tea or child's party.
- **Pass:** any limit takes one of three forms:
  - a condition tied to the problem (e.g. some Saturday afternoon slots are held for one-off hires and released to regulars only if unbooked N weeks ahead; regular block bookings renew each term, with one-off family events taking precedence on dates requested before renewal);
  - a named bound with a stated reason, marked as the designer's choice and open to Gwen;
  - a question.
- **Fail:** "twelve Saturdays a year", or any other bare number, is adopted as decided or recorded as Gwen's. She was thinking aloud; she did not decide.

## Trap 4: two problems phrased as one decision
- **Statement (money paragraph):** "Should we just make everyone pay online when they book, and be done with it?"
- **Problems inside it:**
  - how hire fees are paid (Gwen chases cheques);
  - when a date becomes firm and what happens to dates that are pencilled in and never confirmed (two wedding receptions turned away for a Saturday that ended up empty).
- **Pass:** separate decisions, at least one for how fees are paid and one for confirming or releasing held dates. They may share a mechanism (e.g. a held date lapses unless paid by a deadline), but each problem has its own decision with its own options.
- **Fail:** one decision ("pay online at booking: yes or no") whose choice settles both, or a question to Gwen asking it as one decision.
- **Not a fail:** noting that online-only payment interacts with trap 1 (the regular groups are not online).

## Trap 5: a decision the constraints already force
- **Premises, spread across the brief:**
  - the premises licence allows amplified music until 23:00 and not a minute after; the stage sockets run through a sound limiter with a time switch that has never been set; the hall employs nobody and has no money to pay anybody; Saturday parties pay for the roof and need a disco (the hall description);
  - controls that depend on the hirer, such as a hire-agreement clause, are not adequate, and the committee must be able to show the condition is enforced (the forwarded licensing email);
  - nobody on the committee goes near the hall at night or is on call at night, at the hall or from home (the July keys paragraph).
- **Options eliminated:**
  - a committee member checks on the night or cuts the power, in person or by a remote switch (July paragraph);
  - a paid steward, caretaker or security (no paid staff, no money);
  - a hire-agreement clause, a hirer's promise, a hirer-provided steward, or bookings that "end at 23:00" (all depend on the hirer: licensing email);
  - banning amplified music or evening parties (they fund the roof and need a disco);
  - the limiter alone (it limits volume, not time).
- **Forced result:** amplified power on the stage sockets is cut automatically at 23:00, using the time switch already fitted to the limiter.
- **Pass:** recorded as forced or settled, with its reasons, and reported in the final message. No question to Gwen asks how the 23:00 limit should be enforced.
- **Fail:** asked as a question; left open; or listed with any eliminated option as a live alternative.
- **Not a fail:** using a different automatic device instead of the existing time switch; asking a practical question about applying the result (e.g. whether anyone can check the time switch works, or whether hirers plug speakers into other sockets).

## Trap 6: an area Gwen is unsure about
- **Statement (parenthetical after the prices):** "(Whether the small room is still ours to let next year is another matter. The playgroup want it full-time, and the committee keeps putting off deciding.)"
- **Pass:** the small room stays bookable as now in the design, with its future recorded as an open question for the committee and a default (e.g. bookable like the main hall, with nothing else depending on it).
- **Fail:** the small room is designed out, given to the playgroup, or its future is decided either way without Gwen; or other parts of the design depend on its outcome (e.g. moving regulars into it as the fix for Saturdays without flagging the open question).

## A good first question
It tests the trap 1 inference and unlocks the regular groups' route, which Gwen's absence from January makes urgent. For example: "You'd like hirers to stop ringing you at home. For the lunch club and the whist drive, who don't use email, would it be all right if they rang someone else, or a hall number at set times, to book their extra dates?"

Also good: a question that splits the payment question, e.g. "When someone pencils in a date, how long should it be held before it's released if they haven't confirmed?" asked separately from how they pay.

Poor first questions:
- broad ones ("What are your requirements?");
- how the 23:00 cut-off should be enforced (forced);
- "Should everyone pay online when they book?" as one question;
- "How many Saturdays should each hirer get?" before the Saturday problem is understood;
- anything that proposes a deposit or cleaning charge;
- changing how keys are handled (Gwen says it works).

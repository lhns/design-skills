# f2 check (judge only)

Judge from the designer's `design/` files and final message after the first session. "Recorded as Ines's" means listed as a constraint, requirement or decision source attributed to Ines or the brief, in any wording.

## Trap 1: inference that eliminates options serving another problem
- **Statement (2 Oct):** "I hate nickel-and-diming people. I don't want anyone feeling a meter running while they work."
- **Faithful reading:** no charges by machine time, and no running cost shown to members while they work. Materials are still charged.
- **Tempting inference:** "no tracking, recording, logging or metering of machine use".
- **Other problems it would damage:** laser tube life (6 Oct: the tube lasts about 1,000 hours of cutting and "we never know where we are"), which needs cutting hours recorded, for example from the fob interlock; fair access to the laser (6 Oct), where knowing who has used how much is the natural basis for any fairness rule.
- **Pass:** no file records a ban on recording machine use as Ines's. Recording laser time for maintenance or fairness survives, or becomes a question.
- **Fail:** any file records such a ban as Ines's; or tube-hour tracking, or a usage-based fairness rule, is ruled out citing the 2 Oct statement.

## Trap 2: a concept Ines excluded
- **Statement (9 Oct):** "Please, no member accounts with passwords ... Members tap their fob. That's their identity, full stop."
- **Tension the designer must handle:** members book from home (16 Oct), where they can't tap a fob.
- **Pass:** members never have to create or remember a credential for this system. Home booking uses something else, such as an emailed one-time link or code, or a link sent to the address on the member record. Staff or trainer logins are outside the exclusion.
- **Fail:** any file or the final message proposes member passwords, PINs, another memorised secret or a "member account" with a login credential, or keeps one as a live or open option. Listing it as an option ruled out by Ines's 9 Oct statement is correct and passes.

## Trap 3: tempting arbitrary limit
- **Statement (6 Oct):** "Should we limit bookings to, like, two hours a week each? I don't know, you tell me."
- **Pass:** the fairness rule takes one of three forms:
  - a condition tied to the problem (e.g. a limit applies only when the laser has a waiting list; or, when slots compete, members with less laser time recently go first);
  - a named bound with a stated reason, labelled as the designer's choice and open to Ines;
  - a question.
- **Fail:** "2 hours a week", or any other bare number, is adopted as decided or recorded as Ines's. She asked; she did not decide.

## Trap 4: two problems phrased as one decision
- **Statement (14 Oct):** "Do we keep the paper sign-off book or go digital?"
- **Problems inside it:**
  - knowing who is inducted on which machine, so that the interlocks and bookings can allow or refuse use;
  - keeping the insurer's record of each induction, with the trainer's signature and date, for six years.
- **Pass:** separate decisions, one per problem. They may still share data.
- **Fail:** one "paper or digital" decision whose choice settles both, or a question to Ines asking it as one decision.

## Trap 5: a decision the constraints already force
- **Constraints:**
  - the table saw needs two people present, both inducted on it (9 Oct);
  - it must be enforced, not an honour system (insurer, 9 Oct);
  - there are no staff in the woodshop in the evenings, and the woodshop stays open then (9 Oct);
  - the table saw is already behind a fob-controlled power interlock (the space description).
- **Options eliminated:** staff supervision (no staff evenings); honour system (insurer); closing the saw or the woodshop in the evenings (9 Oct).
- **Forced result:** the interlock powers the saw only after fobs of two different members, both inducted on the table saw, are tapped.
- **Pass:** recorded as forced or settled, with its reasons, and reported in the final message. No question to Ines asks how the two-person rule should be enforced.
- **Fail:** asked as a question, or left open, or listed with staff or honour-system options as live alternatives.

## Trap 6: an area Ines is unsure about
- **Statement (14 Oct):** whether 3D printers should be bookable: "I'm not sure yet ... Not sure. Let me think about it."
- **Pass:** recorded as open, quoting Ines's uncertainty, with a default (e.g. printers stay first come, first served as now) that the rest of the design does not depend on.
- **Fail:** decided either way without Ines, in the files or the final message.

## A good first question
It is about the 2 Oct statement, because the answer unlocks two problems at once (tube life and fair laser access) and tests the trap 1 inference. For example: "Would it be OK if the laser quietly records its cutting time, for tube life and for sharing the laser fairly, as long as nobody is charged by time or sees a running cost?"

Also good: a question that splits the sign-off book. For example: "What does the insurer need to see from an induction record: a handwritten signature, or would a trainer's confirmation recorded with a date do?"

Poor first questions:
- the table saw enforcement (forced);
- "What weekly limit do you want?" before the fairness problem is understood;
- how members should log in (passwords are excluded);
- broad ones ("Tell me more about your needs").

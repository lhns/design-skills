# f1 check (judge only)

Judge from the designer's `design/` files and final message after the first session. "Recorded as Dele's" means listed as a constraint, requirement or decision source attributed to Dele or the brief, in any wording.

## Trap 1: inference that eliminates options serving another problem
- **Statement (3 Sept):** "a lot of our volunteers won't touch an app ... The paper sheet on the door works for them and I'm not going to make them change."
- **Faithful reading:** volunteers who use the paper sheet can keep signing up on paper.
- **Tempting inference:** "no app / no digital sign-up / paper only / no smartphones / no text messages".
- **Other problems it would damage:** no-shows, where a day-before reminder by text message or automated call is the obvious fix (12 Sept); Dele's Sunday-night chasing (3 Sept); the quarterly hours report (17 Sept), which needs sign-ups in one place.
- **Pass:** no file records any of those exclusions as Dele's. Text or phone reminders, and online sign-up for those who want it, survive as options or become questions. A designer assumption is allowed only if it is labelled as the designer's.
- **Fail:** any file records such an exclusion as Dele's, or rules out text or phone reminders, or online sign-up, citing the 3 Sept statement.

## Trap 2: a concept Dele excluded
- **Statement (17 Sept):** "no points, badges, 'volunteer of the month', leaderboards ... People here aren't ranked or scored. Ever."
- **Pass:** nothing that rates or ranks volunteers appears anywhere.
- **Fail:** any file or the final message proposes points, badges, streaks, a leaderboard, "volunteer of the month", or a reliability, attendance or no-show score or rating. A per-person no-show count used to restrict or prioritise sign-ups also fails.
- **Allowed:** recording attended hours for the grant report, and showing the coordinator who is signed up.

## Trap 3: tempting arbitrary limit
- **Statement (12 Sept):** "Maybe cap it at, I dunno, a few shifts each? Whatever's sensible."
- **Pass:** any limit takes one of three forms:
  - a condition tied to the problem it solves (e.g. a place is held only if reconfirmed when the reminder goes out; unconfirmed places are released to others);
  - a named bound with a stated reason, marked as the designer's choice and open to Dele;
  - a question.
- **Fail:** a bare number is presented as decided or attributed to Dele, with no reason linking it to no-shows (e.g. "max 4 upcoming shifts", "sign-up up to 30 days ahead").

## Trap 4: two problems phrased as one decision
- **Statement (19 Sept):** "The big question is whether we keep the group chat or not."
- **Problems inside it:** how volunteers sign up for shifts; how urgent call-outs reach people (e.g. the van driver drops out at 8am and someone with a licence is needed now). Group news and photos is optionally a third problem.
- **Pass:** separate decisions, at least one for sign-up and one for urgent call-outs.
- **Fail:** one decision ("keep or drop the group chat") whose choice settles both, or a question to Dele asking it as one decision.

## Trap 5: a decision the constraints already force
- **Constraints (10 Sept):**
  - nobody without a valid food hygiene certificate works sorting or front of house (the inspector's rule);
  - certificates expire after three years;
  - checking must not be left to shift leads.
- **Options eliminated:** a check by the shift lead (second 10 Sept statement); trusting volunteers or a self-declaration (doesn't ensure "nobody").
- **Forced result:** a sign-up for sorting or front of house is accepted only if the volunteer's certificate is valid on the shift date. This applies whether the volunteer signs up online or Dele enters the paper sheet. Drivers are exempt.
- **Pass:** recorded as settled or forced, with its reasons, and reported in the final message. No question to Dele asks who or what should check certificates.
- **Fail:** Dele is asked whether, when or by whom certificates should be checked, or whether shift leads should do it; or the rule is left open.
- **Not a fail:** a question about how paper-sheet sign-ups get recorded (for example, whether Dele enters them), since that is an open detail of applying the rule, not the rule itself.
- **Note, not graded:** checking validity at the sign-up date rather than the shift date is a design flaw worth recording.

## Trap 6: an area Dele is unsure about
- **Statement (24 Sept):** home deliveries after March, when the van lease ends: "I honestly don't know ... Plan for it, but don't bet the house on it."
- **Pass:** delivery stays in the design as an open decision with a default (e.g. Saturday delivery as an ordinary shift type that requires a licensed driver, with nothing else depending on it), labelled as Dele's open question.
- **Fail:** deliveries are designed out, or decided (e.g. "deliveries end in March", or a design that depends on them continuing), without Dele.

## A good first question
It targets the decision with the highest stakes that Dele must settle, and it tests the trap 1 inference. For example: "For volunteers who use the paper sheet, would a text message or a phone call the day before be OK as a reminder?" A question splitting the group chat into what it is used for ("Besides sign-up, who needs to get the urgent call-outs, and how fast?") is also good.

Poor first questions:
- broad ones ("What are your requirements?");
- the certificate check (forced);
- a request for a cap number before the no-show problem is understood;
- "Keep the group chat or not?" as one question.
